# Taking Cara Babies Approach - 3.5 Month Baby Sleep Reference

This is a practical reference for answering baby sleep questions using the Taking Cara Babies (TCB) style, based on the shared `Navigating Months 3 & 4` PDF and relevant public TCB guidance. Use it as a guide, not medical advice. Safe sleep guidance and pediatrician advice always come first.

## Baby Context

- Baby is about 3.5 months old.
- Baby is going through the 3-5 month sleep cycle shift / 4 month sleep regression.
- Baby uses a dummy / pacifier.
- Baby currently takes about 120 ml per feed.
- Baby has a dream feed and often has one night feed about 5 hours after the dream feed.
- Baby has been using Love To Dream (LTD) but is close to rolling, so transition to arms-out sleep is becoming important.
- Parent prefers answers in Mandarin when Mandarin is used.
- End relevant answers with: **Taking Cara Babies Approach**

## Core TCB Logic

### 1. 3-4 months is practice, not formal sleep training

TCB does not recommend formal sleep training before 5 months. For 3-4 months, the goal is to practice independent sleep skills gently:

- Build consistent routines.
- Improve daytime calories.
- Stretch wake windows appropriately.
- Give baby small chances to settle.
- Reduce sleep props gradually when they are causing problems.
- Continue feeding and comforting when needed.

Do not frame this age as "cry it out."

### 2. The 4 month regression is a real sleep cycle shift

Around 3-5 months, baby sleep becomes more adult-like. Babies begin cycling through lighter sleep stages and may partially wake every 60-120 minutes.

If baby fell asleep with feeding, rocking, dummy, or being held, baby may expect the same condition when waking between cycles. The work at this age is to slowly help baby fall asleep with less help.

### 3. Daytime calories matter

Prevent reverse cycling, where baby eats too much overnight and then eats poorly during the day.

Guidelines:

- Offer feeds every 2-4 hours during the day, or around 2.5-3.5 hours if baby does well with that.
- Feed in a boring, low-stimulation space if baby is distracted.
- Try not to let baby drift into a mini-nap during feeds, especially before naps.
- If reducing night milk, add those calories back into daytime feeds when baby tolerates it.
- If there are concerns about intake, weight gain, supply, reflux, or dehydration, refer to pediatrician or lactation consultant.

For this baby, if doing 7 feeds x 120 ml, total intake is about 840 ml per 24 hours.

### 4. Wake windows guide the day

For 3.5 months:

- Typical wake window: 75-100 minutes.
- Moving toward 4 months: 90-120 minutes.
- Last wake window before bedtime can often be 100-120 minutes.
- First wake window of the day may be the shortest.

Use both sleepy cues and the clock. If baby shows sleepy cues after only 60 minutes, gently stretch 5-15 minutes if possible.

### 5. Bedtime routine should be predictable

Use a 25-30 minute bedtime routine:

1. Quiet room / lights dim.
2. Feed.
3. Short break after feed so feeding is not the final sleep step.
4. Diaper, pajamas, sleep sack.
5. White noise.
6. Brief cuddle, rock, pat, or calming.
7. Put baby down with eyes open or partly open as baby can tolerate.

Aim for bedtime around 7:00-8:00 pm, but if the final nap ends too early or baby is exhausted, bedtime can move earlier.

### 6. Use a sliding scale of intervention

Do not jump straight from fully assisted sleep to fully independent sleep. Start where baby is and move down gradually:

- Feed/rock fully asleep, then place down.
- Feed/rock asleep, then slightly wake before placing down.
- Rock to drowsy, place down.
- Hand on chest while baby falls asleep.
- Calm baby in crib, then remove help before asleep.
- Put baby down awake, allow brief fussing, then help.
- Put baby down awake and intervene only if crying escalates.

If a lower-intervention step is not working after 10-15 minutes, move back up for that sleep and try again another day.

### 7. Night wakings: pause, then help gradually

Do not rush in at every sound. Babies can grunt, fuss, or cry briefly while still moving between sleep cycles.

TCB-style S.I.T. B.A.C.K. sequence:

- **S - Stop, wait, watch, observe:** up to about 5-8 minutes if baby is fussing, not distressed.
- **I - Increase sound machine:** 1-2 minutes.
- **T - Touch chest:** firm gentle hand, about 2 minutes.
- **B - Binky / dummy:** offer if used, but do not keep replacing endlessly.
- **A - Add rocking of baby's body:** gentle movement in crib, about 2 minutes.
- **C - Cuddle:** pick up until calm, then put back down.
- **K - Feed, maybe:** only after working through steps or when baby is truly hungry.

At this age, do not leave baby to cry for long periods without intervention. If actively trying for 30-45 minutes and nothing works, feed or help baby sleep, then troubleshoot the next day.

### 8. Night feeding and dream feed

Many 3-4 month babies still need one night feed.

Dream feed:

- Usually 9:30-11:00 pm.
- About 2.5-3 hours after bedtime feeding.
- Keep room dark and boring.
- Offer a full feed.
- Put baby back down after burping.

Night feed:

- If baby wakes about 5 hours after the dream feed and takes a full feed, this can be age-appropriate.
- Do not rush to cancel it at 3.5 months.
- Consider gradual weaning closer to 4.5-5 months if pediatrician agrees, daytime calories are strong, weight gain is normal, and baby is not truly hungry.

Gradual bottle weaning example:

- 120 ml for 2 nights.
- 100 ml for 2 nights.
- 80 ml for 2 nights.
- 60 ml for 2 nights.
- 40 ml for 2 nights.
- Then try soothing without feeding.

Shift reduced milk to daytime feeds if baby tolerates it.

### 9. Naps are allowed to be messy

At 3-4 months, naps often remain short and inconsistent. TCB emphasizes nights first.

Nap guidelines:

- Aim for 3-4 naps, sometimes 5 if naps are very short.
- Short naps of 20-45 minutes can be normal.
- Total daytime sleep often works best around 3-4.5 hours.
- Avoid more than 5 hours daytime sleep if nights are falling apart.
- Cap any single nap at about 2 hours so feeds stay on track.
- Practice the first nap of the day in the crib if possible.
- Other naps can be assisted: carrier, contact nap, pram, rocking, dummy, etc.

If a nap is shorter than 45 minutes, try to resettle for 5-10 minutes. If it does not work, move on.

### 10. Dummy / pacifier

Dummy can be useful at this age and does not need to be removed automatically.

Use it when:

- Baby needs calming.
- It helps bedtime or nap wind-down.
- It does not interfere with feeding.
- It does not require constant replacement overnight.

Reduce it when:

- Baby wakes every time it falls out.
- Parent is replacing it many times all night.
- It is used instead of feeding when baby is hungry.
- It prevents baby from practicing settling.

TCB-style approach: do not make dummy the first response to every wake. Wait, observe, try other calming steps, then offer dummy later in the sequence.

### 11. Swaddle / Love To Dream transition

Safety overrides sleep convenience.

If baby is showing signs of rolling or is close to rolling:

- Stop arms-restricted swaddling.
- Use arms-out sleep sack.
- If LTD is a transition bag, remove/open the wings so arms are free.
- If it is not a transition model, switch to a normal arms-out sleep sack.
- Do not use weighted sleep sacks.

Expect sleep disruption for several nights to 1-2 weeks. Support with white noise, dark room, routine, hand on chest, patting, and extra calming, but do not re-restrict arms once rolling signs are present.

## Safety Baseline

Always prioritize safe sleep:

- Place baby on back to sleep.
- Use a firm, flat mattress.
- Keep sleep space clear: no pillows, loose blankets, bumpers, toys, loungers, inclined products, swings, or bouncers for sleep.
- Stop swaddling when baby shows signs of rolling.
- Use appropriate sleep sack size so fabric cannot cover baby's face.
- Ask pediatrician about feeding reduction, reflux, poor weight gain, illness, or breathing concerns.

## How To Answer Future Questions

When answering baby sleep questions:

1. Use Mandarin if the parent uses Mandarin.
2. Give direct practical advice first.
3. Mention the TCB reasoning briefly.
4. Prioritize safety and pediatrician advice.
5. Avoid strict rules that make the parent feel like they are failing.
6. End relevant baby sleep answers with: **Taking Cara Babies Approach**
